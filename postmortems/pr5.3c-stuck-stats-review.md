# Postmortem: PR5.3c, stuck, stats, sizes and schema version 11 (its one review) (PR #117)

PR5.3c gives an operator three commands over one queue, `stuck`, `stats` and `sizes`, on five new reads of `OperatorReads`, with the index of schema version 11. One review of the branch's head found no HIGH finding, one MEDIUM and seven LOW. The MEDIUM is a product defect: a run that is due and that the claim's own requirement refuses was in no leg of `stuck` at any grace, so `stuck --fail-if-any` exited 0 for a run that no tick will ever take, while `stats` counted the run as due and `explain` called its task stuck. Six of the eight count by the repository's rule. The verdict: every one of the six was found by outside review, the defect that matters was a consequence the plan had written down as intended, and the check that was built to hold the finder to the engine could not see it, because it asked the question from one side only.

**This document is adversarial toward the MACHINERY and blameless toward
people.** Never "who wrote it", "should have noticed", "was careless" — those
explain nothing and are not actionable. Always "what would have made this
unwritable, or caught it without a human looking". Every section below asks a
question whose comfortable answer is the wrong one; if a section is easy to
fill in, it has not been answered yet.

## Severity

Nothing shipped: the pull request is a draft, and the review ran before any merge. What would have shipped without it, worst first.

A command whose name is `stuck`, with a flag made for a monitor, that stays silent for the one kind of run nothing will ever move (finding 1). The legs held what the engine's next claim or sweep would take. A due run that the claim refuses is taken by neither, so it was in no leg, at a grace of zero or of an hour. The review's state was one task whose stored retry strategy is not JSON, an hour after it came due: a claim took nothing, a sweep took nothing, `stuck --grace 0s --fail-if-any` listed nothing and exited 0, `stats` printed `pendingRunsDue` 1 with a claim lag of the hour, and `explain` printed `pending-due-unclaimed` with the verdict `stuck`. An operator who watched the queue through `stuck --fail-if-any` would have seen green for as long as the row stood. Only `--older-than` found it, by its age. No engine path writes such a row, and a row no engine path writes is what an operator's command is for. The engine does write one run a claim refuses, the due run of a task past its cancellation deadline, and that task was listed, as a task the sweep cancels.

A list that skipped its first row (finding 3). `eventWaiters` read 1,001 waits in the order of run and step, core sorted those by task and cut to 1,000. With more than 1,001 waits on one event, the wait that the read's order put last could be first in the order the list printed, and it was left out, while DESIGN.md promised the first 1,000 in the order of task, run and step. `explain` prints that list. It takes an event with more than a thousand waits to see it.

A gate that excused more than it said (finding 6). The measured surface under the libSQL plan reader holds the reader to a measurement: a statement that grows beside a backlog must be refused by the reader, or excused. On main the excuse was a due range that drives another step. This branch made it any due range the plan read at all, and its BUILD.md entry and its `gate-changes:` line both called the change a tightening. A variation of a shipped statement that grew while it read a lone range on any of four columns, through any index and with or without a LIMIT, passed both of the oracle's properties. No such variation exists on the branch, so nothing wrong passed. What was lost was the oracle's power to catch the reader passing one.

Three statements that said more than the code. DESIGN.md said a live task whose enqueue instant is outside its bounds is listed however old was asked. It is reported only when its leg reads it, and a task enqueued past its bound is the last row of its state, so for such a row what `stuck --older-than` exits with depends on `--limit`: 0 for a limit of one, 10 for two and for five (finding 2). The comment on what `stats` prints said a queue whose every run waits on an event nobody emits is `quiet`, and it is `active` (finding 7). The doc of `nextWakeAtMs` said it is the earliest instant of any row a gauge counts, and the enqueue instant of a live task is not among them (finding 8).

## Recurrence

A corrupt row read as a clean one recurred, in finding 1, and it has now appeared in every round that built an operator's read. PR5.3b1's round met it as an event with no instant read as an ordinary event and as a rogue state printed hidden, and built a generated NULL case for every persisted integer of four tables and one definition of what `inspect` cannot read. PR5.3b2's round met it as a rogue state that `explain` named nothing for, and built one function that `inspect`'s exit and `explain`'s cause both read. Both mechanisms work on a value a read selects: core decodes it, and what the decoder refuses is named in `corrupt`. That is what they check. What they were supposed to hold is that no operator's read passes over a row the engine holds unsafe without saying so. A command that lists rows by the engine's own predicate does not select the value that makes the row unsafe. The predicate reads it inside the database and the row is not answered at all, so there is nothing for a decoder to refuse and no entry to generate a case for. The two earlier mechanisms are a decoder's, and a WHERE clause is not a decoder. This round's leg reaches the rows a claim's requirement refuses. It does not reach a row that another predicate drops, which the last section names.

A hold that could not fail for what it claims recurred, in findings 1, 2 and 3, and it has recurred in every round so far. The differential could not fail for a run no leg lists. The cap case's two orders agreed. The case of an unreadable enqueue instant planted the one direction in which the sentence is true. The mechanism against the class is the mutation registry, and every one of the sixty-five mutations this branch registered was caught. A mutation changes a line of the product. Each of these three lived in what a case was given: which runs a state holds, which ids a fixture writes, which side of a bound a value is on. PR5.3b2's postmortem said the same of its own round, and its record changed nothing here, because it names where the class lives and gives no way to range over a case's inputs.

A gate moved without a true line recurred, in finding 6. PR3.3c's round met a fuzz op that stopped drawing with no line at all, PR3.14c's a test that went from planning every send to planning one with no line, and PR3.14d's a line that said a deleted test's property stays held when two of its holds were lost. Each was answered by writing the line. The rule asks the author to say what moved, and nothing computes it, so a line can be missing, or present and wrong about the direction, as it was here. This is the second time the line was present and false.

A statement that says more than the code recurred, in findings 2, 7 and 8 and in the word of finding 6, and it has recurred in every round of this milestone. Findings 7 and 8 are sentences that a later commit of the same branch made false: the brief asked that each sentence be read against the code when it is written, and these were true then. Nothing reads a sentence against what it describes, and nothing re-reads the sentences a change leaves standing, so the class is open after this round as after the six before it.

## Root cause

The legs were specified by where their rows come from, the engine's own predicates, and every check that was built asked whether a leg's rows are the engine's. None asked whether the engine's predicates cover the rows an operator needs, because the specification had already answered that: a leg means what the engine means. So the differential, the seeded answers, the instants and the walks all compared a leg with the statement it was built from, and they agreed, as two readings of one predicate do. The rows that matter most to the command's name are the rows the engine will not touch, and a check that starts from what the engine takes cannot arrive at them. The gauges were checked from the other side, against a count of a dump, and they were right about the run the legs left out. The two checks stood in one case and nothing compared their answers. The fold compares them for the runs that are due. It does not for the leases and the deadlines, which is the first item of the last section.

The other counted findings share a smaller cause. A case, a sentence or a line was true of the instance in front of its author: ids that happened to sort alike, a value planted on the side of a bound where the sentence holds, a queue as it was before a later commit added a gauge, a change read as "reports more" where it meant "excuses more". Nothing in the machinery asks of a case what input would make it fail, or of a sentence what later change would make it false.
