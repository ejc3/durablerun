# Postmortem: PR5.2d, the purge verb and the simulated week (its one review and the narrow re-review of its fold)

PR5.2d adds `pnpm cli purge`, the first command by which an operator deletes durable state, and the simulated week that holds retention to a bound on three dialects. One review of the branch's head found nothing that lets `purge` delete a unit the barrier keeps, and seventeen other things, four of them MEDIUM: a run that kept a unit its own purge had freed and said nothing remained, a limit that did not bound the work on kept units, a unit in doubt that was named by half its name, and a parser that took a flag given twice. The fold of that review was reviewed once more, narrowly, and that second review found thirteen things, all of them introduced by the fold, two of them MEDIUM. Four of the thirteen, both MEDIUM ones among them, were made by machinery the fold built on a ruling of the coordinator's: a bound on the candidates one run examines, and a cursor to resume from. The second fold took that machinery out and did not repair it. Twenty-one findings count by the repository's rule, and this project's own machinery found none of them.

**This document is adversarial toward the MACHINERY and blameless toward
people.** Never "who wrote it", "should have noticed", "was careless" — those
explain nothing and are not actionable. Always "what would have made this
unwritable, or caught it without a human looking". Every section below asks a
question whose comfortable answer is the wrong one; if a section is easy to
fill in, it has not been answered yet.

## Severity

Nothing shipped: the branch was not merged, and no released package holds the verb. What would have shipped without the first review is a command that deletes and that tells an operator something false about what is left. The worst of it is finding 1. A child that completed under a parent which then failed is listed before its parent. One run kept the child, because its parent could still run again, purged the parent later in the same walk, and ended `finished: true, more: false` with the child standing and the reason `parent-can-run-again` printed beside it, which was no longer true. A schedule that trusts `more: false` leaves such a unit until its next period, and the bound the simulated week showed, one pass of lag, was shown for a walk the verb did not run.

Finding 4 would have purged what an operator did not mean. `purge --completed-after 36500d --cancelled-after 36500d --completed-after 1h --execute` purged under one hour: the parser kept the last value of a flag given twice, and the two required windows are one of the guards against a run that was not meant.

Finding 3 loses a unit from the record. A purge whose batch committed and whose answer was lost was named only by its task id. The row is gone, so no later run lists it, and the count of periods that exit test line 44 will make joins purge reports by the digest of the key, which was in neither run's output.

Finding 2 is a cost and no wrong deletion: at a limit of 1, a run sent 96 batches to purge one unit behind 30 kept ones, and nothing said that the work on kept units grows with the queue.

What would have shipped without the second review is worse than what the first found, and was made by the fold. With the bound and the cursor (findings 13 and 14), a chain of resumed runs ended `finished: true, more: false` with a unit standing that the barrier would let go, which is finding 1 again through two commands, and a schedule with one fixed command line examined the same thousand kept units on every run, purged nothing, and exited 0 every time. An operator would have seen a healthy purge job and a table that grows. Finding 15 let a line break in the cursor forge lines of the text report, `finished: true` among them. Finding 16 dropped a unit out of every list of the report when the read of the barrier in its second try was lost.

The other counted findings are holds that could not fail for what they claimed (findings 7, 8, 17, 18, 19 and 20) and sentences that said more than the code or the record (findings 9, 10, 11, 12 and 21). None of them is a wrong deletion. Each is a place where a later defect would have passed.

## Recurrence

A second copy of one thing recurred, in finding 1, and it is the class the repository's simplify gate exists for. The walk of a queue's candidates was written four times: in the verb, in the week, in the verb's twin and in the fault matrix, each with a page and a stopping rule of its own. The week's walk repeated until it took nothing and the verb's did not, so the property the week showed was not the verb's. PR5.2c2's round met the class as two readings of one value, and the SDK's residual round gave the rule: a single representation, at the source. What the mechanism against it checks is what its author sees. The simplify pass of this pull request saw two of the four copies, the week's and the contest's, and rejected joining them, for a reason that was true of those two: they differ in the page and in when they stop. It did not see that the verb's was a third and that the difference in when they stop was the defect. The gate asks for a pass and a written reason, and a written reason is not a check. The walk is one function now for four of its callers. Two keep walks of their own, the contest and the fuzz shards, each for a reason BUILD.md and the pull request state, and the audit below says what that leaves.

A report that states a read made before a later write recurred, in finding 1's printed reason and in finding 16. PR5.3d's round met it as a cancellation that printed what a read before the write had said. Its mechanism holds that a command sends its port call. It does not hold what a command prints of a read once a later call of the same command has changed what the read was of. Here the walk read why a unit was kept, then purged the unit that kept it, and printed the first reading. The answer is in the walk and not in a rule: a kept unit is looked at again when a take could have freed it, and its entry is replaced by what the second look reads.

A hold that could not fail for what it claims recurred, in findings 7, 8, 17, 18, 19 and 20, and it has recurred in every round so far. The mechanism against it is the mutation registry, and it did its work on what it was given: every mutation the branch registered was caught by its own case, at each head the audit ran on. PR5.3c's round said why that is not enough: a mutation changes a line of the product and cannot range over what a case is given. Findings 7, 17 and 20 are that half again: a control of nothing, lists that were empty on every walk, a count where a name was meant. Finding 18 is a new half, and the plainest of the six. The other side of the comparison became the product. Before the first fold, the twin's port side was written in the test. The fold moved the walk into core and made the twin call it, so the registered mutant of the walk changed both sides of the comparison alike, and the case that was the verb's main hold could no longer fail on what the walk takes. No mutation can catch that, because the audit asks whether a case fails under a mutant, and it does: on the other lines of the case. The model of the barrier, an oracle written from the specification and not from the code, had been in this repository since the pull request before this one, in the conformance package, and held every pass of the simulated week. The verb's own test did not use it until finding 18 said so.

A fix built past the smallest sufficient answer recurred, in findings 13, 14 and 15 and in R6, and this is the recurrence that matters most in this round. PR5.3c's second round found the same shape: windows built to bound a read, with cases that held the bound and none that held what the bound does to an operator's answer. The repository's rule is written down: a finding authorizes the smallest sufficient fix, and does not by itself authorize new machinery. The first review's second finding was a cost that nothing stated. ## Fix-induced defects

Thirteen. Every finding of the second review, R1 to R13, was introduced by the fold of the first, nine of them counted and four not. The second review was of the fold alone, and it found more than the first review had found in the whole branch.

Four of the thirteen, R1, R2, R4 and R6, both MEDIUM findings among them, have one cause, and the cause is a ruling and not a slip in building it. The first review's second finding said that the limit bounds the units taken and not the work on kept units. The coordinator's ruling on it asked for a bound on the candidates one run examines, with a cursor to resume from. That asked for machinery where a statement of the cost was the smallest sufficient answer. A bound on what a run examines needs a cursor, a cursor needs a chain of runs to be right as a whole, and a schedule with one fixed command line then starves behind kept units at exit 0. No line of the exit test asks for the bound. The fold built what the ruling asked, with cases that held the bound and none that held the chain. The remedy was to take the machinery out and not to repair it: the walk lost the bound, the cursor and the place to resume from, the verb lost `--after`, and the cases and the four mutations that held only those went with them. What stands in their place is a page that is no longer the limit, a statement in section 3.11 of what one run costs and that nothing bounds it but the queue, and the bound recorded in BUILD.md as an option, with what building it taught: a resumed run must return to the kept units a later take freed, and a fixed command line must not starve behind kept units.

R9 was also made by a ruling: the fold was asked to make the walk one function that the twin runs too, and the twin then compared the walk with the walk. The remedy there was a different oracle and not a withdrawal.

The other eight were made in building the fold. R5 is a list edited before the look that should have settled it. R8, R10 and R12 are holds weakened by moving the walk: the lists a repeat compares became empty, the matrix's faults landed in the dry walk, and a case came to expect the answer's own list. R3, R7 and R11 are costs and a copy that the move brought. R13 is a sentence.

Two more defects were made by a fix and caught by the fold's own checks before any review, and are not counted: the cursor's task id reached the port unchecked, which a new case of the first fold found, and four replacements of registered mutants dropped a question mark their finds hold, which the registry's self-test refused.

Were the fixes reviewed as new code? The first fold was: the narrow re-review read it as a change of its own, and that is what found thirteen. The second fold was not. It is the last, and no review follows it. It was re-tested, by what the evidence section lists. That is the weaker of the two answers, and this round is itself the evidence of how much weaker: a fold that was tested green was then read, and thirteen things were found in it. The second fold is smaller in what it adds. It removes more than it builds, its one product fix landed red first, and the largest thing it builds is a test. It is still a change made straight after understanding something, and nobody has read it.

## Root cause

The verb was held to itself. Each hold that should have caught a counted product defect compared the command with something made from the same code. The twin replayed the command's own calls in the command's own order, and after the first fold ran the command's own walk. The case of a run's batches expected the answer's own list. The repeat compared a list with itself, empty. The week that showed the bound ran a different walk from the one an operator runs. A check built that way passes whatever the code does, as long as the code does it on both sides, and each of findings 1, 13, 17, 18 and 20 is that sentence.

The thing to hold it to was already in the repository. The barrier has a model, the model has an oracle written from it, and the simulated week holds every one of its 131 passes to that oracle. The command's tests are in another package, and were written from the port upward: is the command the port's calls. That question is answered by a twin. The question an operator has is another one: when the command says nothing remains, is that so by the model. It was not asked of the command until the second review.

The second cause is the size of the folds. The first review's fold changed where the walk lives, what one run is, what its report holds and how the parser reads a command line, and added a bound and a cursor on top. It was the largest change of the pull request and it was made as a repair: its cases were written against the findings it answered, and the properties it disturbed, the matrix's cells and the twin's comparison among them, had no case that would move. The machinery it added came from a ruling, so nothing weighed it against the rule that asks for the smallest sufficient fix, and the rule is prose that no check reads.

## What this round still would not catch

A defect of the walk on a state no case is given would ship. The model is asked only about the states the tests build, and the audit planted a walk that never tries again a unit a held outcome kept: the verb's test passed, and one case of core, over a port written by hand, is all that fails it. A new way for one take to free another unit, in a later schema or a new kind of task, has no case until someone writes the state.

A walk that sends far more batches than it needs would ship. The audit ran one: 1,201 purges where 601 are needed, with every case green.

A purge pass that is not the operator's pass can still be shown for it. The contest of four purgers and the fuzz shards keep walks of their own, so a property held there is held of those walks. The chain of runs is held alone on its database: no case runs the command to its end beside a spawner or a second purger.

A fixture whose turn does nothing would ship on PostgreSQL and MySQL, and a control week that holds one row of each table passes the vacuity check.

A fault at a second look, or two faults in one run, is not armed by the matrix.

A list the port comes to read, exported unfrozen or as a Set, passes the case of the frozen lists until its name is added.

A value with a line break still writes lines of a text report, in `sizes` and `inspect` today and in any command that prints a string an operator typed.

A sentence that is false of the code would ship, in the design, in the plan, in a pull request's text and in this document. This round found one in its own record by reading, after two reviews had read past it.

And a fix that builds more than the finding asks would be built again. The rule against it is prose, the ruling that broke it was read by its author and by the builder, and the only thing that caught the result was a second review, which this fold does not have.
