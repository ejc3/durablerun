# Postmortem: PR3.14c plan check review

PR3.14c adds a check to the libSQL store's plan test. One scripted history of real operations sends every statement the store ships, the generated SQL corpus and the list of text statements hold that history to "every", and a reader judges the loop nests of each statement's plan: a step that runs once for each row of another must be keyed, and the step it runs once for each row of must be keyed or a due range. One review found nothing HIGH, two MEDIUM and six LOW findings, and seven of the eight count. Nothing the store ships was wrong. What was wrong was the check: it passed a shape it could have refused, three of its holds could not fail, and its documents claimed twice what was not so.

**This document is adversarial toward the MACHINERY and blameless toward
people.** Never "who wrote it", "should have noticed", "was careless" — those
explain nothing and are not actionable. Always "what would have made this
unwritable, or caught it without a human looking". Every section below asks a
question whose comfortable answer is the wrong one; if a section is easy to
fill in, it has not been answered yet.

## Severity

No statement the store ships is affected, and no user would have seen anything. The worst finding is that the check was blind in one place it could see: a plan step that reads a subquery's rows was never judged, so a statement that scans a materialized subquery once for each run of a backlog would have merged with the check green. A check that is believed and blind is how the scans this work exists to stop got in before it. The rest would have shipped a list whose reasons nothing held, a reader that did not fail closed where its documents said it did, and a plan that said no earlier test planned a read when three do.

## Recurrence

A name standing where an identity belongs recurs. AGENTS.md keeps a catalogue of proxies that were paid for, and it holds a checker that found its subjects by filename and lints that matched one spelling. This change's own simplify pass removed the resolution of step names to tables, on the argument that a step is judged by its constraints whatever it names, and left the one place where a name still decided: the map of body names, global to the plan. The earlier mechanism against the class is a standing instruction to ask whether a mechanism is the property or a picture of it. An instruction cannot fail, so it did not.

A hold that cannot fail recurs. The rounds of PR #63, PR #65 and PR #72 each counted at least one, and PR #72's counted three. Nothing general asks of a new hold what input makes it fail, so every new surface meets the class afresh. In this change a control was watched failing for the rule test and for the registered mutation, and none was for the list of names or for the claim that the reader fails closed, and those are exactly findings 4 and 5.

A sentence that is not true recurs. The rounds of PR #63 and PR #72 each counted sentences of the documents that were false, and this count follows the same rule. This round institutes no mechanism against the class, because none exists at any rung here.

## Fix-induced defects

One of the seven. Finding 3 was introduced inside this pull request by its own simplify fold, the commit 79be1d5 on this branch, which made the history keep one send of each statement so that the two older tests plan each distinct write once. That fold was proved by comparing the reading of every statement before and after, which cannot see a send that is no longer planned. It was re-tested and not re-reviewed as new code until this review read it.

The same is true of this round's own fixes, and it is said here so that nobody assumes otherwise. The two commits that change the reader were each held by their commit guard to identical readings of all 128 statements this branch's own base ships, before and after, and no second review was planned, on the maintainer's stated condition that the reader's fix stayed the fall through and the scoping and that those readings did not change. They are re-tested, not re-reviewed.

## Root cause

The reader's evidence was what it passes. Its main proof was the 128 plans the store then shipped, every one of which it must accept, and its refusals were exercised by eight statements picked by hand. Every finding about the reader lives where no shipped plan goes and no picked case went: a body inside a nest, a line SQLite does not print today, a word after the constraint list. AGENTS.md has the law for this, that a new layer gets a generated fault surface of its own at birth. The reader is a new layer whose units are plan lines and nests, and its surface was chosen by hand. The reviewer's 29 probes were that surface, made once, by a person.

The other three findings share a second cause. A sentence about what tests do, and a change to what an untouched test iterates, are both outside anything a test in this repository reads. The first can only be caught by a reader of prose, and the second only by an author who thinks to declare it.

## Mechanisms

Built in this PR:

- A read of a body is judged against what drives it as a read of a table is, and a body's name is known only to the select that holds its line. Rung 3, in `packages/store-libsql/test/plan-nests.ts`, held by four cases.
- Every send of every statement is planned, and all sends of one text are held to plan alike, with a control that a plan does change with a bound state. Rung 3, in `packages/store-libsql/test/query-plans.test.ts`.
- Each statement in which a due range drives pins its driving lines and a bound the test checks. Rung 3, the LIMIT half syntactic, in the same file.
- A row under no line of the plan, lines under a sort line, and a body or an index leg with no step are faults, and the constraint list is read wherever it stands. Rung 3, syntactic, in the reader.

Deferred (recorded in BUILD.md):

- A generated surface for the reader: every kind of line it knows, as the step that drives and as the step that is driven, under each reach, with the expected reading derived and not written by hand. It is the mechanism the root cause names. Deferral is acceptable because the reader guards a test of one dialect, nothing the store ships reaches the shapes it would add, and the entry records its trigger, the next finding against the reader.

## What this round still would not catch

A keyed body of many rows read inside a nest would pass today, as would any nest in which both steps are keyed and one entity's rows are many. A statement that plans otherwise under binds the history never sends would pass. A named statement whose LIMIT stands somewhere other than over the range that drives would pass, and so would another nest under a driving line of the same text. A new plan line that begins like a sort marker would be skipped, and a parenthesised group before the constraint list would be read as the list. Any false sentence about what a test does would ship, and so would a cleanup that narrows what an untouched test iterates, unless its author thinks to declare it. And everything DESIGN.md already lists stays true: a due range under no limit, a range that points away from what is due, a lone walk in a read or an INSERT ... SELECT, and a statement inside a trigger.
