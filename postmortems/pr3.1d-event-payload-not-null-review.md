# Postmortem: PR3.1d event payload NOT NULL review

PR3.1d makes every dialect's schema refuse SQL NULL as an event's payload, as schema version 10: a declared NOT NULL on PostgreSQL and MySQL, and two triggers on libSQL, where SQLite cannot add the declaration to a column that exists and a measured rebuild of the table was refused for what it costs. An await that times out answers with no payload, so a stored NULL would make a waiter read a delivered event as a timeout. The pull request's one review, the built-in code review at high effort with the coordinator's reviewer reproducing on servers of its own, found nothing HIGH, five MEDIUM and ten LOW. Its one narrow second review, of the fold alone, found nothing HIGH or MEDIUM and seven LOW. Eleven are counted here: ten from the first review, and one from the second, a false claim that the fold itself wrote. None lets the engine's own port store a NULL at the reviewed head. Two are doors that only a foreign session or a future dialect could walk through, one is a figure in the documents that an operator would have trusted, and one is a checker that had stopped firing anywhere. All eleven were found by the two reviews and none by the author's machinery.

**This document is adversarial toward the MACHINERY and blameless toward
people.** Never "who wrote it", "should have noticed", "was careless": those
explain nothing and are not actionable. Always "what would have made this
unwritable, or caught it without a human looking".

## Severity

The worst is finding 1, because it is the one an operator meets in ordinary use. Every libSQL figure in the documents (90 to 725 ms, no call of a worker beside it failed) was measured right after the table was built, from the page cache. Rows of `events` are written once and rarely read again, so a real table is cold. On a cold file of 4.5 GB the check of the rows already there scans inside the version's write transaction for 12 to 15 seconds, and another connection's calls fail beside it: its first write waits out the executor's 5 second busy timeout, and most of the rest fail at once, by an older defect that finding 11 describes. The failure is loud and it ends with the scan, and an operator who trusted the documents would have met it with no warning and no remedy.

Finding 2 is the only one that can corrupt. MySQL refused a row that already holds NULL only because of the strict mode the executor sets on its own sessions. In a session without it the shipped statement succeeded and stored an empty string where the NULL was, with a warning nobody reads, and a waiter would then read a delivered event whose payload is not JSON. No session of this engine is such a session. A port in another language replays the version's text and not the executor's session setup, and the contract says such a port is a drop-in peer.

Finding 3 would have let a future dialect ship a schema that refuses an UPDATE of NULL and accepts an INSERT of it while passing everything the dialects share. Finding 4 left an invariant arm that no test on any dialect saw fire. Finding 9 let a mistyped caller of MySQL's guarded form record its version over a column that never changed, with no caller that can reach it today. The other five are documents that said less than holds, or restated something by hand: the servers' lock queues and MySQL's rebuild (finding 5), three stale counts, one export missing from the body's list, one cause given for two kinds of failed call, and a test that re-spelled a constant of the schema.

Finding 11 came from the second review, and the fold caused it. To correct finding 8 the fold wrote that a worker's writes failed once the five second busy timeout ran out and that each read failed behind a failed write. The author's own log refutes that by arithmetic, because the worker is one loop and 216 failed writes do not fit inside a rebuild of 48.5 s at five seconds each. The second review measured what does happen, and the BUILD.md entry the fold had written for the defect was narrower than the defect: it named reads only, and the red test it proposed would have passed a fix for reads alone.

The table numbers the first ten in the first review's order: its points 1, 2, 3 and 5, then its points 6, 7 and 13 as one, then 10, 11, 12, 14 and 15. Finding 11 is the second review's finding 1. Three of the review's fifteen points are not counted. Point 4 is a test on main that pins the newest version as a literal 9, which this range does not hold and the merge re-aims. Point 8 weighs PostgreSQL's three-transaction form, which is recorded as an option and is not a defect. Point 9 is the transient error type of a version refused over a NULL row, which the permanent store error work types on main and this pull request asserts after its merge. Six of the second review's seven findings are not counted either, because each is an incomplete claim at most, or a nit: the second cause of MySQL's error 1846, three names that say "while nullable", three conditions missing from the cold figures, one sentence about a rolling deploy that overstated, one refusal in test support with no case, and two ragged wraps. All six were folded.

## Recurrence

Three classes, and two of them have been here before.

A figure or a cause in a document that its own measurement does not carry: findings 1, 5, 8 and 11. This class has recurred in every round on main that measured something. The keyed write locks round quoted a timing with no log behind it, and the lesson recorded was to keep the log and name the measure. PR4.4b's round wrote counts that were true at seven versions as constants. PR4.6's round had two MEDIUM findings where DESIGN.md claimed more about version 7 under live traffic than was measured, and its postmortem ends by saying that a sentence of DESIGN.md that says more than was measured would ship today. It did. This branch kept its logs, named its measures and listed its neighbours, and still shipped figures whose one unstated condition, a warm cache, was the one that mattered. The mechanism so far is a lesson, and a lesson is not a rung. What it checks is that a figure has a log. What it was supposed to check is that the log's conditions are the ones the claim will be read under. The fold's own first cold control shows how thin the lesson is: the script that was written to measure a cold file warmed it, and what caught that was reading the cache's state right before the timed call, which is an assertion a script can make and a lesson cannot. Finding 11 is the same class a second time inside this round: the fold's correction stated a cause that the log it was drawn from refutes by arithmetic, and 27 green gates carried it to the second review.

A proxy where the property fits: findings 2, 3 and 4. AGENTS.md keeps the catalogue, and these are three more entries. The executor's session state stood in for the statement's own refusal, and the mutation registered for it removed the session state, which proves that the session state matters and says nothing of the statement. One refused UPDATE stood in for the schema refusing the value. A count of declared conditions stood in for a checker that fires, which is the class PR3.10's per-condition mutation work was planned against, after two conditions of the wake surface could be deleted with all 1,728 cases green. That work stays deferred, so its mechanism was never built, and the completeness test that called this arm covered is the same test.

A second copy: finding 10, and findings 6 and 7 in prose. The single representation law dates from the SDK round, and PR4.4d hoisted the third copies of four kinds into core and the conformance package. A test's copy of an unexported constant is outside what either reached, because nothing enumerates what a test restates.

## Root cause

The branch proved each property along the path the engine itself takes, and every finding is a state that arrives by another path. The refusal was asserted through the executor's session, and the defect was in a session the executor did not set up. The shared case sent the one statement the fixture already had, and the defect was the write form it did not send. The migration was timed right after the script that built the table, and the defect was a file nobody had read that day. The servers were measured beside the engine's own short transactions, and the queue forms behind a foreign transaction that is older. The guard was run over the real column, and it failed open over a name the catalog lacks. The machinery samples the engine's own path well: a shared red, eleven write forms on libSQL, generated crash cuts, mutations caught exact-only. It has no generator for the states a foreign writer, another port, an operator or a cold disk brings, so each of those was found by someone who thought to bring it.

- PostgreSQL's three-transaction form (a CHECK added NOT VALID, validated, then SET NOT NULL), with the reviewer's numbers and its cost of three versions under the present runner.
- A short lock timeout on both servers' migrations, which PostgreSQL's rule 11 already weighed and which now names MySQL's `lock_wait_timeout`.
- PR3.15, a libSQL write that fails busy fails the calls that follow it on its connection: it is being built as its own pull request, which rewrites its BUILD.md entry when it merges, and it is not part of this change.
- PR3.10's per-condition mutations stay deferred, and finding 4 is the second time their absence was paid for.

## What this round still would not catch

- A later MySQL version that changes a column with a bare ALTER, written by hand and not through the guarded form, would store a default over a NULL in a session with no strict mode, and every test would pass, because the case that holds the clause names version 10.
- A dialect whose schema refuses a NULL payload by something narrower than the column, such as a trigger or a check that looks at another column too, would pass both doors of the shared case, because the doors are two statements with fixed values.
- An invariant condition whose every witness is refused by every schema is called covered by the completeness test from the day it is declared, whether or not any test sees it fire.
- A guarded column form aimed at the wrong column, one that is already NOT NULL, records its version and changes nothing.
- A figure or a cause in DESIGN.md that its own measurement does not carry, whether it was measured in a state its reader will not be in or drawn from a summary that cannot show it, or a count restated from a pin, would ship today. This is the fourth round on main to say so, the first three did not stop this one, and inside this round the fix of one such cause wrote another.
